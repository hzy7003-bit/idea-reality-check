# Research Playbook

## Search from the problem outward

Build queries in several forms: the plain-language problem and situation; the job or outcome desired and workaround language; the mechanism, parts, and functional verbs; synonyms, older category names, colloquial terms, and translations; and failure, complaint, repair, discontinued, recall, review, and comparison terms.

Translate key concepts into Chinese and relevant target-market languages (often English, Japanese, and Korean; add others as warranted). Literal translation may miss local vocabulary, so search local category terms and marketplace categories too. Search exact phrases and broader concept variants. Record representative queries and date.

## Market and product coverage

Choose sources for the problem and target user. Make a compact region/source matrix and state coverage rather than implying an exhaustive census.

- China: official manufacturer/product pages, Taobao, JD, Pinduoduo and other accessible listings, Chinese patent records, Bilibili, Zhihu, and relevant forums or communities.
- United States: official products, Amazon, Kickstarter/Indiegogo, Reddit, YouTube, trade sources, and US patent/regulatory records.
- Europe: manufacturers and retailers in relevant countries, crowdfunding, user communities, EPO/WIPO records, and applicable regulators.
- Japan and South Korea: local-language product pages, major retail/marketplace listings, patent registers, and local video/community sources.
- Software: GitHub, package registries, app stores, product changelogs, pricing pages, issue trackers, and user communities.
- Industrial or service ideas: buyer/vendor pages, procurement material, trade publications, standards, case studies, and direct interviews when available.

Search marketplaces, crowdfunding, GitHub, Reddit, YouTube/Bilibili, and forums when they fit the category. If a source is blocked, inaccessible, or unhelpful, record that limit and use another route. Do not imply every listing is representative.

Classify existence carefully: concept/patent; prototype/demo; crowdfunding/preorder; launched for sale; continuing availability; discontinued; or verified production/adoption. A live product page is not proof of current stock or sales. A crowdfunding campaign is not proof of delivered units. Confirm launch or discontinuation with dated first-party or credible historical evidence where possible.

For user experience, prefer identifiable direct reviews, issue reports, repair reports, demonstrations, and multiple independent accounts. Note region, date, model/version, and context. Do not generalize from one review.

## Patent and prior-art search

Start with Google Patents for discovery and use official national or regional registers for authoritative records. Relevant sources may include CNIPA, WIPO PATENTSCOPE, USPTO, J-PlatPat, and EPO Espacenet/register. Search multiple languages and mechanism terms; inspect drawings, independent claims, cited patents, patent families, and later filings.

Record what the document teaches, filing/publication dates, jurisdiction, applicant/inventor, and status shown by a register. Patent family and legal-status data can be incomplete or delayed. Avoid saying an idea is patented or protected everywhere based on one document. Avoid legal conclusions about infringement or freedom to operate.

Patent evidence answers whether similar subject matter was disclosed or pursued. It does not answer whether users want the product, it was built, or it succeeded. Resume the product and adoption search after a patent hit.

For regulations and standards, use the official regulator/standards body for the intended market. Check publication status, effective date, transitional rules, and the product/vehicle category covered; a newly published standard may not yet be in force. If jurisdiction, vehicle, or intended use is missing, state the assumed scope and leave model-specific compliance unresolved rather than giving a blanket legal conclusion.

## Evidence ledger

For each key finding, keep:

| Claim | Evidence/source | Region/date | Evidence type | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What is actually supported | Direct link | Where/when it applies | Official record, product, review, demonstration, etc. | Missing context or coverage |

Use source quality proportionally:

1. Official product, company, patent, regulator, standard, or public filing for what that source controls.
2. First-hand user reviews, repair reports, demos, or interviews for a specific experience.
3. Credible reporting and trade publications for context and history.
4. Search snippets, directories, aggregators, and discussion summaries as leads to verify.

A company source can establish its advertised feature, not independent performance. A marketplace listing can establish an offer, not sales volume. A patent can establish publication, not commercial success.

## Failure and adoption research

Search product names and inventors with discontinued, failure, recall, repair, replacement parts, complaint, review, teardown, weight, fit, leakage, battery, maintenance, and revision. Compare variants over time. Seek causes from dated product history and direct evidence.

Treat explanations like “too expensive,” “users did not want it,” or “manufacturing was difficult” as hypotheses until supported by sourced prices, product changes, reviews, company statements, production information, or other relevant evidence. If no reliable explanation surfaces, report the cause as unknown.

## Reporting gaps

State which regions, languages, marketplaces, registers, or communities were searched and which were inaccessible or not searched. Phrase a negative result as “I did not find a matching product in these sources with these queries,” never “no such product exists,” unless unusually strong authoritative evidence supports that statement within a defined scope.
